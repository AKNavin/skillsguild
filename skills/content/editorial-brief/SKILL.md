# Editorial brief builder

Create a brief that makes the reader's need, article angle and evidence requirements clear before drafting.

## Define the piece

Capture the topic, audience, intended reader outcome, publication channel and business objective. Propose a specific angle if the request is broad, labelling it as an editorial choice.

Identify the reader's likely starting knowledge and the questions the piece must answer. Distinguish questions inferred from the brief from questions supported by supplied audience or search data.

## Build the brief

Provide a working title, central argument, scope boundaries, ordered outline, examples needed and a source plan. Explain what each section contributes; avoid multiple sections repeating the same pitch.

For factual claims, specify the evidence needed and prefer primary sources. If current research is requested and browsing is available, retrieve sources and report what they actually support. Never invent source URLs, keyword metrics, customer quotes or product capabilities.

## Deliverable

Return a writer-ready brief with angle, audience, outline, evidence checklist, voice and length constraints, and acceptance criteria. Add outstanding decisions only when they affect the draft. Keep promotional claims proportional to supplied evidence and make the call to action fit the reader's task.
