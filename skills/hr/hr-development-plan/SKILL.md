# Individual development plan

## Use
Use when an employee and manager want a practical development plan for an agreed capability. Keep career aspirations employee-stated rather than inferred.

## Inputs
Chosen growth goal, current examples, target role expectations, weekly time and available support. Use private `hr-context.md` for coaching style and `role-brief.md` for scope. If the goal is vague, propose a small choice of observable capabilities for agreement.

## Do
1. Describe the gap as a work task the employee wants to perform more effectively. Separate capability gaps from missing tools, authority or workload capacity.
2. Select one to three development outcomes. Establish a baseline work sample and the evidence that would demonstrate progress.
3. Pair each outcome with deliberate practice on real work, a feedback partner and only the learning resources needed. Do not default every gap to a course.
4. Allocate practice time and manager support. Show dependencies and a fallback if the stretch assignment is unavailable.
5. Schedule checkpoints and a final demonstration. Treat course completion as participation evidence, not proof of capability.
6. Update the plan from supplied progress notes, keeping action IDs and employee choices. Record suggested changes without promising promotion or pay changes.

## Deliver
`development-plan.csv`: outcome_id, target_task, baseline_evidence, practice_action, resource, time_budget, support_owner, checkpoint, success_evidence, status.
Add a brief coaching note and the next practice assignment.

## Check
Actions fit the stated time, every outcome has observable evidence, and the employee can tell exactly what to do next.

## Example
“Build a six-week plan for a new manager who wants better delegation, using two hours a week and a fortnightly mentor session.”
