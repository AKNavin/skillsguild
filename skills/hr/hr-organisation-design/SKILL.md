# Map organisation design options

Create a role-level organisation design options paper. Use business strategy, current role/decision map, workflow bottlenecks, capacity and constraints. Analyse work and accountability rather than selecting people for removal.

## Method
1. Define the problem and observable success measures: handoff delays, duplicated decisions, unclear ownership or unmet capability. Distinguish documented friction from stakeholder opinion.
2. Map current functions, reporting relationships, critical decisions, interfaces and demand. Flag missing owners and circular accountability; do not assume a reporting line proves decision authority.
3. Develop a small set of viable role/team structures including the unchanged baseline. For each, explain decision rights, cross-team dependencies, capacity assumptions and transition demands.
4. Compare options against the agreed measures, costs and constraints using explicit evidence. Do not apply generic span-of-control targets as universal rules or score individual employees.
5. Produce a transition sequence with role clarification, consultation requirements for specialist review, systems changes, communications and evaluation dates. Separate proposal, approval and implemented state.

## Deliver
Current/proposed role diagrams; `decision | accountable_role | contributors | handoff | escalation`; options table with benefits, trade-offs, assumptions and evidence; owner-assigned transition plan.

**Check:** each critical decision has a clear accountable role, required work has capacity, and proposed headcount/cost changes reconcile. Uncertain legal, consultation or employment consequences require the authorised HR/legal owner; the output is not a redundancy selection list.

**Try:** “Compare two support-team structures that reduce escalation handoffs, retaining a baseline and showing accountability and transition costs.”

Source: [CIPD organisation design](https://www.cipd.org/uk/knowledge/factsheets/organisational-development-design-factsheet/). Checked 2026-10-14; jurisdiction-specific guidance is not a universal legal rule.
