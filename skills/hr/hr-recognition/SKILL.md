# Specific recognition drafts

## Use
Use when a manager or HR wants to acknowledge a specific contribution. Create personal, factual appreciation rather than a generic award or automated employee ranking.

## Inputs
Contribution, observed impact, contributors, preferred recognition channel and any approved reward rules. Use private `hr-context.md` for tone and examples of accepted phrasing. Ask about public versus private recognition if that preference is unknown.

## Do
1. Verify what happened, who contributed and the source of the claimed impact. Remove invented metrics and unsupported superlatives.
2. Link the contribution to a relevant team outcome or stated value using a concrete example. Include enabling or less-visible work when the supplied evidence supports it.
3. Draft a concise message naming the action, why it mattered and thanks. Match the recipient's known preference without inferring personality from their name or background.
4. Check confidentiality: do not reveal customer identities, individual cases, health details or commercially sensitive outcomes in a public message.
5. If an award or payment is requested, prepare the nomination with the published criteria and approved limit. A human selects recipients and authorizes expenditure.
6. Maintain a minimal recognition log so duplicate messages are avoided. Review aggregate coverage by team or work type only when authorized; absence of recognition is not evidence of poor performance.

## Deliver
Two message options and, if relevant, an award nomination draft.
`recognition-log.csv`: event_id, contribution, evidence_ref, contributors, intended_audience, preference_confirmed, draft, approval_status, sent_at.

## Check
Credit is accurate, impact is supported, privacy preferences are respected, and nothing is published or paid without authorization.

## Example
“Draft a short private thank-you for the payroll team resolving this reconciliation issue, using my usual direct style.”
