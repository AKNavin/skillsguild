# Plan workplace wellbeing actions

Plan an organisational wellbeing initiative from permitted aggregate evidence. Focus on work conditions and support; do not diagnose individuals, infer mental health from messages or generate employee risk scores.

## Inputs and work
Use aggregated employee feedback, workload/process evidence, available support, owner capacity and a budget. Optional HR context supplies organisational priorities. Keep health disclosures outside general planning files.

1. Define the problem and evidence quality. Separate reported concerns, measured workload patterns and hypotheses. Suppress small groups and identifiable comments under the organisation's reporting policy.
2. Prioritise practical changes to work design, manager support, schedules or access to existing resources. Do not substitute a generic wellness activity for an identified workload or process issue.
3. Develop actions with owner, cost, dependency, success measure and review date. Label assumptions and avoid guarantees of health or productivity outcomes.
4. Draft accessible communications explaining voluntary support, confidentiality limits and contact routes. Do not collect new health information merely to measure participation.
5. Compare later aggregate results with the stated baseline and participation/response limitations. Report uncertainty; improved survey scores alone do not establish causation. Urgent individual safety concerns go to the established immediate-response channel.

## Deliver and check
`workplace_issue | evidence | action | owner | budget | due | measure | review_date`, plus communication drafts and evaluation plan. Each action must address an evidenced workplace issue, have an owner and avoid individual diagnostic claims.

**Try:** “Build a practical workload and manager-support plan from this anonymised employee feedback and our existing resources.”

Source: [CIPD health and wellbeing at work](https://prod.cipd.org/uk/knowledge/factsheets/well-being-factsheet/). Checked 2026-10-14; jurisdiction-specific guidance is not a universal legal rule.
