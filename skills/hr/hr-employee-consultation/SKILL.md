# Coordinate employee consultation

Prepare and administer consultation on a proposed workplace change under the organisation's authorised process. Do not treat an announcement of a final decision as consultation or determine legal obligations without jurisdiction-specific verification.

## Inputs and work
Use the proposal, rationale, affected groups, applicable locations, collective agreements, representative arrangements and responsible legal/HR owner. Preserve what is open for influence and what is already constrained.

1. Ask the owner to verify whether a statutory, contractual or agreed process applies, including participants, representation, timings and required disclosures. Do not import another country's thresholds or assume an ordinary meeting satisfies it.
2. Draft an accessible explanation of the proposal, alternatives, possible effects and practical questions. Clearly label it proposed; avoid language suggesting feedback cannot affect the result.
3. Prepare invitations, agenda, response channels and timeline with authorised recipients. Address conflicts of interest and confidential material through the designated owner.
4. Record feedback faithfully, including alternatives and unresolved objections. Separate identifiable case information from shareable themes; do not infer agreement from silence or attendance.
5. Create an owner response log linking each issue to consideration, evidence, proposed change or reason for no change. The accountable human records any final decision and subsequent communications.

## Deliver and check
Consultation pack and `issue_id | source_group | proposal_or_concern | response_owner | evidence | considered_change | response_status`. Check that material concerns are traceable and no draft is labelled complete merely because a meeting occurred.

**Try:** “Prepare a consultation pack for this proposed shift-pattern change and a tracker for employee and representative feedback.”

Source: [Acas consulting employees (UK)](https://www.acas.org.uk/consulting-employees). Checked 2026-10-14; jurisdiction-specific guidance is not a universal legal rule.
