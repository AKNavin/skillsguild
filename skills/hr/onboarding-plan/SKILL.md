# 30–60–90 day onboarding

Turn role expectations into a practical ramp from supported work to independent ownership.

## Inputs

Use the confirmed start date/timezone, working pattern, responsibilities, manager and buddy, available documentation, tools and realistic first projects. Optional private `role-brief.md` defines outcomes and stakeholders; `hr-context.md` supplies communication style and check-in preferences. Record the version of both. Missing resources are gaps, not facts to invent.

## Workflow

1. Separate organisation-wide orientation from team context and role-specific capability. Confirm calendar days versus working days for 30/60/90 milestones; respect holidays and existing commitments.
2. Before arrival, identify unresolved access and preparation dependencies. Days 1–30: select one bounded contribution, essential relationships and feedback rhythm. Days 31–60: specify a supported project. Days 61–90: define a credible ownership scope and joint review.
3. Give each milestone an observable result, owner, support contact and evidence. Replace “understand the business” with a work sample or demonstrated workflow. Keep targets provisional when expectations are unconfirmed.
4. Produce the first-week agenda without filling every hour. Include focused work, essential introductions and a manager check-in; use the starter's stated preferences without inferring ability or personal circumstances.
5. Update the existing plan by stable milestone ID. Preserve completed work and record changed dates with reasons. Draft reminders for owners; do not schedule or send them automatically.

## Deliver

`onboarding-plan.csv`: milestone_id, phase, outcome, activity, owner, due_date, dependency, evidence, status. Add first-week agenda, manager check-in prompts and resource gaps.

## Check

Every milestone connects to a role outcome and available support. Distinguish onboarding progress from performance ratings; completion is not probation approval.

## Try it

“Use our senior designer role brief and existing onboarding checklist to create a 30–60–90 day plan. Keep completed orientation items and give the manager a first-week agenda.”
