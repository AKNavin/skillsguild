# Role-based goals

## Use
Use when setting or refreshing individual or team goals. Produce a draft that the employee and manager can agree, rather than assigning a performance rating.

## Inputs
Role responsibilities, planning period, business priorities, baseline evidence and available capacity. Read private `hr-context.md` and `role-brief.md` if supplied; use the approved terminology and communication style. Mark missing baselines as unknown.

## Do
1. Map each responsibility to the business outcome it supports. Propose three to five priorities; identify work that should stop or wait to fit capacity.
2. Choose an outcome metric, formula, source and owner. Distinguish individual control from shared dependencies. For new or uncertain work, use learning milestones and evidence of progress instead of inventing numerical targets.
3. Show baseline, proposed target and rationale separately. Include a quality guardrail so speed or volume cannot mask poor work. Never invent a baseline from an aspiration.
4. Add dependency owners, resource needs, checkpoints and a rule for revisiting goals when scope changes.
5. Draft a short agreement note with unresolved decisions. Retain the prior version and show changes when refreshing goals.

## Deliver
`goals.csv`: goal_id, role_outcome, baseline, metric_formula, target, evidence_source, quality_guardrail, owner, dependency, due_date, checkpoint, status.
Add `goal-agreement.md` with scope, tradeoffs, open questions and manager/employee confirmation fields.

## Check
Every goal has observable evidence and a feasible review date. Targets remain proposals until agreed; no protected-attribute assumptions or ratings enter the goal sheet.

## Example
“Turn this support-lead role brief and Q4 priorities into four goals, showing missing baselines and weekly checkpoints.”
