# Coordinate workplace adjustments

Use for administrative coordination of an employee's requested workplace adjustment. Do not diagnose, assess disability eligibility or decide approval.

## Inputs and work
Use the person's stated request, relevant job tasks, work barriers, local procedure and authorised case owner. Keep medical material in its designated restricted system; the general task tracker needs no diagnosis.

1. Record the request as stated, received date, preferred contact method and immediate practical needs. Do not require a medical label before routing it to the owner.
2. Prepare neutral questions about the work barrier and possible support. Distinguish requested changes, employer suggestions and agreed arrangements. Obtain only information the authorised process requires.
3. Draft options for the human owner to discuss with the employee: equipment, access, scheduling, communication or task support. Do not decide feasibility, entitlement or hardship from limited data.
4. Track the agreed action, implementation owner, follow-up date and evidence that it is working. A pending decision is never a rejection. Local requirements and specialist advice determine process and time limits.
5. Produce a minimal manager-facing action note that omits medical details, and a separate restricted chronology. Sending either requires authorisation for the intended audience.

## Deliver and check
`request_id | work_barrier | requested_support | owner | next_contact | agreed_action | implementation_status | review_date`. Include a factual acknowledgement draft and unresolved questions. Check that no diagnostic inference, automatic decision or unnecessary disclosure appears; employee feedback and owner confirmation establish completion.

**Try:** “Organise this adjustment request into a confidential tracker and a practical manager action note.”

Source: [EEOC reasonable accommodation guidance (US)](https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/small-employers-and-reasonable-accommodation). Checked 2026-10-14; jurisdiction-specific guidance is not a universal legal rule.
