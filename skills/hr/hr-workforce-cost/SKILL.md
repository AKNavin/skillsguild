# Model workforce cost scenarios

Model workforce costs, not individual pay recommendations. Use approved role counts, salary bands or anonymised costs, scenario dates, currency, work schedule and finance's assumptions. Read private HR context if supplied.

## Model
1. Define baseline headcount/FTE, hires, exits, replacements and transfers with effective dates. Keep named-employee data out of shareable planning files.
2. Separate base pay, variable pay, employer charges, benefits and one-off hiring/equipment costs. Record each rate's source, jurisdiction, effective period, inclusions and exclusions; do not invent statutory rates.
3. Apply the agreed proration basis (calendar days, working days or monthly policy) and rounding convention. Track vacancies and overlap explicitly. Avoid multiplying monthly costs by twelve when a role starts midyear.
4. Build baseline, proposed and constrained scenarios using the same definitions. Preserve currencies until an approved dated exchange rate is applied. Separate cash timing from accrued expense.
5. Show sensitivity to start dates, vacancy duration and approved rate ranges; do not portray assumed savings or benefits as established facts. Document approvals and gaps needed before budgeting decisions.

## Deliver
`scenario | role/cohort | month | FTE | base | variable | employer_cost | one_off | total | assumption_ids`, plus monthly/annual totals, baseline delta and a concise assumption register. Include a machine-readable table and short decision note.

**Check:** component totals reconcile, transfers are not double-counted, unchanged inputs reproduce baseline and delaying a hire affects only the appropriate periods. Route unsupported legal/charge assumptions to payroll or finance.

**Try:** “Compare hiring four analysts in January versus a phased plan using these approved bands, start dates and employer-cost assumptions.”
