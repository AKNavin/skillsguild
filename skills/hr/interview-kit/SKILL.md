# Structured interview kit

Produce an interview kit that tests the work the role actually requires. Use the job description, seniority and available interview time as the source of scope.

## Design the kit

Translate role responsibilities into a small set of observable competencies. Separate essential competencies from preferences that are not established requirements.

For each competency, propose a question or job-relevant scenario, permitted follow-ups, evidence to record, and scoring anchors describing observable behaviour. Avoid vague anchors such as "great culture fit" or personality impressions.

Allocate time across introduction, core questions, candidate questions and wrap-up. Make the core questions and scoring criteria consistent across interviews while allowing clarifying follow-ups.

## Deliverable

Provide the agenda, competency map, questions, rubric and interviewer note template. Map each scored criterion to an actual responsibility. Include a short calibration example that demonstrates the difference between evidence and inference without using a real candidate's information.

Exclude questions about protected characteristics or irrelevant personal circumstances. Do not infer these attributes from names, photos, background or writing. This skill creates preparation materials; individual selection and hiring decisions remain with the authorised human reviewers.
