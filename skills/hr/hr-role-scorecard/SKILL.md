# Role evidence scorecard

## Inputs
Use the agreed role outcomes, level, sample work and assessment stages. Optional private `role-brief.md` supplies scope; `hr-context.md` supplies terminology and formatting. An unapproved role requirement stays proposed.

## Workflow
1. Select a small set of distinct capabilities needed at entry. Link each to a role outcome and an observable action. Separate essential requirements from desirable experience and post-joining learning.
2. Define what evidence could demonstrate each capability: a documented example, structured question or short work sample. Assign one primary assessment stage to reduce repetitive interviews.
3. Draft behavioural anchors describing incomplete, sufficient and stronger evidence for the specified level. Add `not observed` as a separate evidence state, never a failing score.
4. Identify evidence that must not substitute for capability: school/employer prestige, rapport, accent, irrelevant career gaps, presumed personality or protected characteristics. Do not infer sensitive traits.
5. Include one synthetic example per ambiguous anchor for reviewers to calibrate. Record rubric version, owner and proposed changes before use. Never alter standards to match a recruiter's preferred style or a particular applicant.

## Deliver
A reusable table: `criterion_id | role outcome | essential/desirable | evidence requested | anchor descriptions | primary stage | reviewer`; plus an evidence-note template containing `source, observation, uncertainty, clarification_needed`. Human reviewers apply the rubric and own selection decisions; this skill does not generate candidate scores.

## Check
Every criterion traces to a responsibility. Anchors differ through observable work rather than adjectives. No criterion is double-counted, and an unasked question produces an evidence gap rather than an adverse conclusion.

Example: "Create a role scorecard for a mid-level payroll specialist with clear evidence standards and no generic culture-fit criteria."
