# Performance support plan

## Use
Use to draft a supportive plan when documented work expectations are not being met. Do not decide discipline, dismissal, disability status or whether formal action is justified.

## Inputs
Applicable policy and jurisdiction, role expectations, dated examples, employee response, earlier support and available resources. Optional private `role-brief.md` defines relevant work; `hr-context.md` supplies tone. Keep individual records private.

## Do
1. Separate the expected standard from observed work and its impact. Mark disputed or unsupported assertions; do not convert labels such as “bad attitude” into facts.
2. Identify questions about training, tools, unclear priorities, workload or access. Route any disclosed accommodation or health issue to the authorized specialist without diagnosis or unnecessary detail.
3. Draft a small number of improvement outcomes using achievable, role-relevant evidence. Pair each with employer-provided support, a responsible owner and a reasonable proposed timeframe.
4. Include the employee's perspective, a check-in cadence and a way to raise obstacles. Distinguish informal coaching from a formal process; follow the approved process without inventing consequences.
5. Prepare the discussion and written record. A human confirms the plan and any employment implications. Do not auto-start a PIP from a metric threshold.
6. At each review, compare new evidence to agreed expectations, record support actually delivered and preserve disputed points. Leave the outcome to the responsible reviewer.

## Deliver
Restricted `support-plan.csv`: expectation, baseline_example, employee_response, improvement_evidence, support, support_owner, checkpoint, review_date, observed_progress, unresolved_issue.
Add a meeting draft and human-review fields.

## Check
Every expectation has evidence and support; resources precede evaluation, and missed targets do not trigger automatic discipline.

## Example
“Draft an informal four-week support plan from these documented service-quality gaps and the employee's response.”
