# Candidate sourcing plan

## Inputs
Use role criteria, target start date, permitted channels, budget and prior campaign results. Read optional private `role-brief.md` and `hr-context.md`. Prior success patterns are hypotheses, not a reason to restrict the search to similar people.

## Workflow
1. Translate the role into essential skills, adjacent titles, transferable experience and relevant work artefacts. Separate true geographic or working-pattern constraints from convenient preferences.
2. Build narrow and broad search variants using platform-supported syntax. Explain each required term, synonym and exclusion. Avoid protected traits, age proxies, school prestige and personal circumstances.
3. Choose a small channel mix: internal mobility, referrals, professional communities, job boards or authorised talent pools as relevant. Estimate effort and cost; label reach assumptions rather than inventing market counts.
4. Create a short experiment for each channel with search query, owner, effort cap, review date and useful measure such as eligible responses per sourcing hour. Use measured results to revise queries without changing role standards.
5. If reviewing supplied profiles, record only job-related public or authorised work evidence and its source. Respect platform access rules and contact preferences; do not enrich with private contact information or infer sensitive characteristics.

## Deliver
A sourcing brief plus `channel | query/approach | role evidence sought | owner | weekly effort | budget | review date | success measure`. Include a deduplication key and a contact-suppression check for later outreach, but do not contact anyone in this planning task.

## Check
Searches can find candidates with equivalent experience under different titles. Requirements trace to the brief; no channel is represented as proven without measured results.

Example: "Plan a two-week sourcing sprint for a backend engineer using our existing channels and a limited recruiter time budget."
