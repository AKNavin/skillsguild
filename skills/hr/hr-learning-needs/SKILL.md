# Learning needs analysis

## Use
Use before commissioning training or building an L&D plan. Identify where learning can address a documented work gap and where another intervention is needed.

## Inputs
Business priorities, role expectations, performance or quality evidence, stakeholder observations and available budget/time. Use private `hr-context.md` for organizational context and `role-brief.md` where supplied. Prefer role or team evidence over unnecessary named employee records.

## Do
1. Define the desired work outcome and compare it with current evidence. Record the period, source and limitations instead of treating a manager's request as a proven cause.
2. Classify each gap as knowledge/practice, process, tools, staffing, unclear expectations or unresolved. Multiple causes may coexist; do not diagnose motivation or personality.
3. For potential learning needs, describe the observable task, current evidence and required demonstration. Seek employee and subject-expert input where the evidence is incomplete.
4. Prioritize using agreed business importance, urgency, affected work and feasibility. Show the rule and reasoning rather than an unexplained AI score.
5. Route non-learning issues to the relevant owner. Estimate effort as a range and distinguish known supplier costs from assumptions.
6. Propose the smallest pilot and how baseline and follow-up work samples will be compared.

## Deliver
`learning-needs.csv`: need_id, role_or_team, target_task, business_outcome, evidence_ref, gap_type, uncertainty, priority_reason, proposed_action, owner, cost_range, success_measure.
Add the ranked backlog and unresolved validation questions.

## Check
Every recommended intervention addresses evidence; process failures are not disguised as training needs, and estimates remain labeled.

## Example
“Analyze these service-quality findings and manager requests into a prioritized learning backlog, separating training from system fixes.”
